Frequently Asked Questions

Technical and general information about the EcoField Logger project

I. Concept & Rationale

Why was EcoField Logger developed instead of using existing tools like KoboToolbox, ODK Collect, or EpiCollect5?

While tools like ODK and Kobo are powerful, they are generic. EcoField Logger was custom-built to align perfectly with the University of Ghana’s biological sciences curriculum. It features a local species database specifically for Ghana’s ecosystems, a group-based system that mirrors student lab groups, and an integrated dashboard that calculates ecological metrics automatically.

What specific problem does the system solve for ecological field research?

It solves the "Connectivity Gap." In remote areas like Shai Hills or Achimota Forest, internet is unreliable. Paper records are easily lost or damaged. EcoField Logger provides a digital, standardized, and offline-capable alternative that ensures data is captured accurately at the moment of observation.

Why was a Progressive Web App (PWA) chosen instead of a native Android/iOS app?

A PWA offers the best of both worlds. It avoids the friction of "App Store" downloads, works on any device (Android, iPhone, or Laptop), and allows for instant updates without user intervention. It is lightweight and highly accessible for students with varying device specifications.

II. Technical Architecture & Offline Mechanics

How does the offline synchronization system work technically?

The app uses a Service Worker to intercept network requests. When a submission occurs offline:

  • Data is serialized and stored in the browser’s IndexedDB.
  • A Sync Tag is registered with the browser.
  • Once internet is detected, the 'sync' event triggers a background process that uploads the stored records to the Flask backend.

What happens if synchronization fails during reconnection?

The system uses transactional logic. A record is only marked as "synced" in the local database after the server returns a successful status code. If the upload fails, the record remains in the "Pending" state and will retry automatically.

How does the system prevent duplicate records during syncing?

Each record is assigned a unique timestamp and ID. client-side logic ensures that once a record is successfully sent, it is flagged as synced, preventing it from being included in future sync batches.

How are photos stored and synchronized while offline?

Photos are converted into Base64 strings (text representation of binary data) and stored in IndexedDB. During synchronization, these strings are sent to the server, decoded back into image files, and saved to the server's filesystem.

What are the storage limitations of IndexedDB in the application?

Storage is determined by the device and browser, typically allowing for hundreds of megabytes. This is more than enough for thousands of text records and several dozen high-resolution field photos.

III. Security & Data Integrity

How secure is the platform?

The platform utilizes HTTPS for all data transfers. Access is tiered: students can only see their group's data via password-protected login, while the Admin panel is isolated behind a separate, secure credential system.

How are passwords stored and protected?

The system uses Bcrypt hashing. Instead of storing actual passwords, the database stores a secure hash. This ensures that even if the database were compromised, actual passwords remain unreadable.

How does the system protect ecological and student data?

Data is maintained through Supabase's secure cloud environment. Student identifiers are kept within a private observations table, and the Archive feature allows admins to move sensitive data to offline storage annually.

IV. Technologies & Tools

What technologies were used and why were they selected?

  • Flask: For its speed and flexibility in building lightweight APIs.
  • Supabase: For robust data management and real-time capabilities.
  • Vanilla JS/CSS: To keep the app lightweight and fast.

Why was Supabase chosen as the database backend?

Supabase provides the power of PostgreSQL with the ease of a cloud API. It allowed for rapid development while ensuring the data is hosted on secure, industry-standard infrastructure.

What is the structure of the database?

The database consists of three primary tables: 'observations' (field data), 'manage_groups' (group credentials), and 'admin_settings' (system configurations).

How are species records organized in the database?

Species are stored as structured text lists. The frontend provides a standardized dropdown based on a master list, while also supporting manual entry for new species discovery.

How are group submissions managed and linked?

Every observation is tagged with a group_id. When a group logs in, the backend filters the data to ensure students only see and manage the records their group collected.

V. Development & Testing

What software development methodology was used during development?

An Agile/Iterative approach was used. The project began with a Minimum Viable Product which was expanded with features like the Admin Dashboard and Photo Uploads based on testing results.

How was the user interface designed for field usability?

The UI follows Mobile-First principles: large buttons for ease of use, high-contrast colors for visibility under sunlight, and a multi-step progress bar to simplify the data entry process.

What testing methods were used to evaluate the system?

The app underwent simulated field testing using browser tools to mimic zero-bandwidth conditions, as well as cross-device testing on Android and iOS to ensure PWA functionality.

Was the app tested in real offline field conditions?

Yes. The app was tested by simulating movement between connected and disconnected zones while performing full data entries and verifying the subsequent sync process.

VI. Compatibility & Scalability

What devices and browsers support the system?

The system supports all modern browsers (Chrome, Safari, Firefox, Edge). It is optimized for mobile devices but works perfectly on tablets and laptops.

What are the current limitations of the system?

The primary limitations include varying Background Sync support on iOS Safari and the potential warm-up delay on the free hosting tier during periods of inactivity.

How scalable is the system for use outside the University of Ghana?

The architecture is highly scalable. The database and species lists can be easily adapted to accommodate any flora/fauna region or different institutional structures.

Can the platform support larger biodiversity or conservation projects in the future?

Yes. The system could be expanded to include Multi-Tenancy, allowing different organizations to have isolated EcoField Logger instances on the same infrastructure.

VII. Impact & Future Directions

How does the system improve ecological data accuracy compared to paper-based methods?

It eliminates transcription errors. By using standardized dropdowns and auto-captured GPS data, the "cleanliness" of the data is ensured at the source.

How does the project contribute to conservation science and ecological informatics?

It bridges fieldwork and data science. By digitizing data at the source, it enables Real-Time Ecology, allowing researchers to see trends as they happen.

What impact could the system have on ecological education and research in Ghana?

It empowers the next generation of scientists with digital literacy, showing that technology is a vital tool for engaging and efficient research.

How does the system improve collaboration among student research groups?

The Stats Dashboard allows groups to see how their data contributes to the collective findings, encouraging comparison and deeper analysis.

What future improvements are planned for the platform?

Future plans include AI-powered species identification, interactive GIS mapping within the app, and direct push notifications for field communication.

Could AI or GIS technologies be integrated into future versions of the system?

Yes. Integrating Google Maps API for spatial analysis and on-device AI for classification would turn EcoField Logger into a world-class professional research tool.
